Step 3 of 5

What type of business entity?

Not sure? Here's a comparison to help you decide. Most small businesses start as an LLC.

LLC (Limited Liability Company)
Recommended

Most popular choice for small businesses. Protects your personal assets, flexible management, and pass-through taxation.

  • Personal asset protection from business debts
  • Flexible management structure
  • Pass-through taxation (no double tax)
  • Easy to set up and maintain
  • Can add members later as you grow
NC Filing Fee: $125
Sole Proprietorship
Simplest

Easiest and cheapest to start, but offers no personal liability protection. Best for very small, low-risk businesses.

  • No state filing required
  • Simplest tax filing (Schedule C)
  • Complete control of your business
  • Minimal paperwork
Filing Fee: $0 (just need EIN)
S-Corporation
Advanced

Better for businesses earning $50K+ in profit. Potential self-employment tax savings, but more complex and expensive to maintain.

  • Self-employment tax savings on distributions
  • Personal asset protection
  • Professional credibility
  • Stock options for future investors
NC Filing Fee: $125 (LLC) + IRS Form 2553
501(c)(3) Nonprofit
Mission-Driven

For charitable, educational, or social missions. Tax-exempt status and eligible for grants, but requires a board and more compliance.

  • Tax-exempt federal and state
  • Eligible for grants and donations
  • Donors get tax deductions
  • Mission-driven credibility
NC Filing: $60 | IRS 1023-EZ: $275
